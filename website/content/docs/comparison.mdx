Three open-source options price derivatives without you writing the numerics: [QuantLib](https://github.com/lballabio/QuantLib) (C++, since 2000), [RustQuant](https://github.com/avhz/RustQuant) (Rust), and stochastic-rs. They overlap less than their descriptions suggest. This page compares feature coverage, including the cases where stochastic-rs is the wrong choice.

## Short version

- **You are modelling volatility** — rough volatility, stochastic volatility, calibration to a smile, path simulation, Greeks by Malliavin calculus. Pick stochastic-rs.
- **You are booking and settling real cash products** across many jurisdictions — convertibles, commodities, exotic coupon structures. Pick QuantLib: its instrument and calendar coverage is far wider.
- **You want a small, dependency-light Rust crate with automatic differentiation** and need only vanilla instruments. Look at RustQuant.

## Where stochastic-rs is ahead

Areas where the other two have little or nothing:

**Rough and fractional volatility.** 132 processes including rough Bergomi, rough Heston, fractional OU, fractional CIR, Volterra kernels and a Markovian lift. QuantLib ships one rough engine (`analyticroughhestonengine`); RustQuant has none. See [processes](/docs/processes).

**Malliavin Greeks.** Thalmaier and El Khatib weight schemes, plus a Fourier-Malliavin volatility estimator. Neither of the others implements Malliavin calculus.

**Statistical estimation.** Hurst exponent (Fukasawa, R/S, DFA, GPH, wavelet, Whittle), maximum likelihood for 1-D diffusions with six transition-density approximations, realised variance with BNHLS bandwidth, a full stationarity battery (ADF, KPSS, Phillips-Perron, ERS-DFGLS, Leybourne-McCabe), HMM, changepoint, particle filter, UKF. QuantLib has descriptive statistics but not econometric estimators. See [statistics](/docs/stats).

**Copulas.** Fifteen bivariate families plus C-vine, D-vine, R-vine and nested Archimedean constructions. QuantLib's `ql/math/copulas` covers the basic families only. See [copulas](/docs/copulas).

**Hardware acceleration.** SIMD sampling on the CPU, and every process on CUDA, Metal or Accelerate. Neither alternative attempts this. See [GPU support](/docs/concepts/gpu-support) and [benchmarks](/docs/benchmarks).

**Native Python.** 308 PyO3 entries, NumPy in and NumPy out, no SWIG layer and no C++ toolchain needed to install. See [Python bindings](/docs/python).

## Where QuantLib is the better choice

**Instrument coverage.** QuantLib has convertible bonds, asset swaps, bond forwards, BMA swaps, range accruals, digital and capped/floored coupons, non-standard swaps, and swing and storage options for energy. stochastic-rs has none of these.

**Calendars and conventions.** Roughly forty holiday calendars against our eight (United States, United Kingdom, TARGET, Tokyo, HKEX, ASX, SGX, B3). To settle a EUR trade against German, French or Italian holidays, QuantLib is the only option.

**Interpolation.** Around twenty-four interpolators including Chebyshev, Lagrange, kernel, convex-monotone variants and 2-D bicubic. We ship four curve interpolators.

**American options at machine precision.** QuantLib implements Barone-Adesi-Whaley, Ju-Zhong and the QD+/QD-FP engines. We have Bjerksund-Stensland 2002, a CRR lattice, finite differences and Bermudan LSM, which is enough for most work but not for the last few basis points.

**Market models.** QuantLib's LMM framework includes products, pathwise Greeks, callability bounds and correlation parameterisations. Our LMM is a drift-coupled simulator without the product layer.

**Institutional familiarity.** Twenty-five years of production use and audit trails count in a regulated setting.

## Where RustQuant fits

RustQuant's distinguishing feature is `RustQuant_autodiff`, an adjoint automatic differentiation engine. stochastic-rs has no AAD — its Greeks come from closed forms, Malliavin weights or finite differences. If gradient-based work is central to your problem and vanilla instruments suffice, that is a real advantage.

It also ships ISO-3166 country codes and ISO-10383 market identifier codes, a small machine-learning module, and CSV/JSON/Parquet data loading. We cover ISO-4217 currencies (162 of them) but not the other two standards, and have no general data-loading layer.

## Known gaps in stochastic-rs

Beyond the instrument and calendar coverage above, the gaps we consider most significant:

1. **No automatic differentiation.** All sensitivities are closed-form, Malliavin or bumped.
2. **No serialisation.** Curves, calibration results and surfaces cannot be persisted; there are no `serde` derives in the workspace.
3. **No netting or collateral layer.** Exposure profiles and CVA / DVA / FVA exist, but there is no netting-set or collateral modelling.
4. **QMC path construction stops at Brownian motion.** `BrownianBridgeQmc` (`stochastic::mc`) builds Brownian levels and increments from a scrambled Sobol sequence, but the processes draw their own Gaussian stream: QMC reaches a model through an explicit Euler loop fed with those increments, not through `ProcessExt::sample`, and there is no PCA path construction.
5. **No commodities.** No seasonal forward curves, no Schwartz-Smith, no storage or swing optionality.
6. **No discrete dividends.** Equity pricing assumes a continuous dividend yield.

## Notes for QuantLib users

| QuantLib | stochastic-rs |
|---|---|
| `StochasticProcess` | `ProcessExt<T>` |
| `PricingEngine` + `Instrument` | `ModelPricer` or `.valuation(curve)`; a small `PricingEngine` + `Instrument` comparison harness also exists |
| `Handle<Quote>` / observers | `market::handle` / `market::observable` |
| `DayCounter` | `DayCountConvention` |
| `Calendar` | `Calendar` + `HolidayCalendar` |
| `YieldTermStructure` | `curves::DiscountCurve` |
| `Schedule` | `calendar::schedule` |

The main structural difference is that stochastic-rs avoids `&dyn` and `Box<dyn>` in hot paths: where QuantLib hands you a polymorphic engine pointer, stochastic-rs uses concrete types and generics, so the engine choice is made at compile time.

## Using them together

They are not mutually exclusive. A common arrangement is QuantLib for the fixed-income and settlement layer and stochastic-rs for volatility modelling, calibration and simulation, both imported side by side from Python.
