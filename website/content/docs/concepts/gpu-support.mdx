Every process carries its sampling backend as a type parameter and is
re-typed with `.on::<B>()` (see [backends](/docs/concepts/backends)). This
page is the inventory: which processes have a device kernel today, on which
backends, and what the rest do.

## The support matrix

| Family | Processes | Capability bound | `Cpu` | `Accelerate` | `Metal` | `Cuda` | `Cubecl` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fGN noise | `Fgn` (1) | `FgnBackend<T>` | SIMD + `ndrustfft` | vDSP FFT | MSL FFT pipeline, `f32` only | cuFFT + Philox, `f32` / `f64` | CubeCL FFT, `f32` only |
| Euler engine, one component | 90 processes | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | Euler–Maruyama kernel in MSL, `f32` only | NVRTC kernel, `f32` / `f64` | CubeCL kernel, `f32` only |
| Euler engine, systems | 35 processes | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | the same kernel, up to four components | the same kernel | the same kernel |
| Euler engine, fractional | `Fbm`, `Fou`, `Fcir`, `Fgbm`, `FJacobi`, `FVasicek`, `Cfgns`, `Cfbms`, `Cfou`, `JumpFou`, `JumpFOUCustom` (11, counted above) | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | fGN pipeline feeds the same MSL kernel | fGN pipeline feeds the same NVRTC kernel | fGN pipeline feeds the same CubeCL kernel |
| Host only | the remaining 5 processes | `HostBackend` | own SIMD sampler | compile error | compile error | compile error | compile error |

The 125 processes on the engine cover the diffusions, the stochastic-volatility
models under both their Euler and quadratic-exponential schemes (`Heston`,
`HestonLog`, `DoubleHeston`, `Heston2D`, `Sabr`, `Bergomi`,
`HestonStochCorr`, `BatesSvj`, `Hkde`), the short-rate models including the
curve-driven ones (`HullWhite`, `HullWhite2F`, `HoLee`, `BlackKarasinski`,
`CirPlusPlus`, `Cir2F`, `DuffieKan`, `DuffieKanJumpExp`, `Vasicek`,
`FVasicek`), the fractional processes, the jump models (`MjdLog`, `BatesSvj`,
`Hkde`, `HawkesJD`, `DuffieKanJumpExp`, and the ones generic over a
user-supplied size law — `Merton`, `Kou`, `LevyDiffusion`, `Bates1996`,
`JumpFou`, `JumpFOUCustom` — whose `ScalarNormal` or `ScalarExp` law the
engine recognises at runtime and draws in the kernel, Bates's as the product
`∏(1 + y) − 1` its multiplicative jump needs; any other law keeps the process
on the host through the same calls), the Lévy processes whose increment
is one draw with no rejection (`Ig`, `Nig`, `IGSubordinator`,
`PoissonSubordinator`, `AlphaStableSubordinator`, `GammaSubordinator`,
`TemperedStableSubordinator`, `Vg`, `BilateralGamma`, `BilateralGammaMotion`,
`Bns`), the discrete-time conditional-variance models at order (1,1)
(`Arch`, `Garch`, `GjrGarch`, `Agarch`, `Egarch`), the first-order lag models
(`ARp`, `MAq`), the noise generators (`Wn`, `Gn`, `Cgns`, `Cfgns`), the
correlated fractional pair (`Cfbms`, `Cfou`), the bounded correlation
processes (`TengSCP`, `VanEmmerich`, `TransformedOU`), the two models whose
coefficients travel as curves rather than parameters (`MultifactorSabr`'s
term structure, `Hjm`'s six time-dependent coefficients), the matrix-valued
models whose rows are independent copies of one small family (`Bgm`'s forward
rates, `Adg`'s affine factors, `WuZhangD`'s rate–variance pairs — one launch
per row), the Cholesky-correlated baskets of up to four streams (`MultiGbm`,
`Mcgns` — one launch, the factor folded into the family's ten lower-triangle
slots; a wider basket stays on the host whatever the backend), the one- and
two-factor `MultifactorHeston` (which rides the double-Heston family with a
zeroed second factor when it has one; three or more stay on the host), the
regime-switching diffusion with up to four regimes (the one-step transition
matrix is exponentiated once on the host and travels as cumulative
thresholds; five or more regimes stay on the host), the Riemann-Liouville
family under the Markov lift of its rough kernel (`RlFBm`, `RlFOU`,
`RlBlackScholes`, `RlHeston`, and `Volterra`'s lift branch — the lift's node
states live in the kernel's own per-path arrays, see below; `Volterra`'s
reference-convolution branch rides the history block instead, the kernel
tabulated on the grid as its weights), the two lift-stepped
Volterra models (`VolterraSquareRoot`, and `GaussianPolynomialVolatility` up
to eight coefficients) and the event-indexed processes in their count mode
(`Poisson`, `CompoundPoisson`, and `CustomJt` / `CompoundCustom` when their
inter-arrival law is the scalar exponential), one arrival per step, and the
models whose step reads their whole past through the history block — the
rough-volatility models with a memory integral (`RoughHeston`, `FBatesSvj`,
`RoughBergomi`), the linear fractional stable motion `Lfsm`, and the ARIMA and
SARIMA recursions at any order, run as the moving-average filter of their
innovations — each on the host's own scheme, for grids up to 512 points; and the
tempered-stable processes built from their shot-noise series (`Cgmy`, `Cts`,
`KoBoL`, `Rdts`), whose terms the series block draws per path and sums into the
grid cells they fall in, the host's sort replaced by addressing, again up to 512
points; and `Hawkes` in its count mode, one event per step by the exact
two-uniform recursion of Dassios and Zhao, the host's thinning loop replaced by
a closed form; `Ctrw` under exponential waits, whose arrivals per cell are the
Poisson count the kernels draw, with normal, Rademacher or symmetric-stable
jumps; and `Lmm` with up to four forwards in one launch, the spot measure's
drift coupling and the reset freezes written out over the four slots; and
`InverseAlphaStableSubordinator`, whose table block builds the direct stable
subordinator per path on its own grid — doubling the extent until it reaches
the horizon, as the host does — and reads the inverse clock off it by
interpolation at every step; and `Svcgmy`, CGMY under a CIR variance, whose
variance takes the exact square-root step — a non-central χ² as the square of a
shifted normal plus the frame's gamma draw — and whose series terms are sized
in the step of their own cell against the variance there, the one series
clause written `live`.

What stays on the host, and why. The engine steps a bounded forward
recursion over four scalar state slots, so what rules a process out is
unbounded work per step, unbounded state, or an output that is not a fixed
grid:

- **A history integral over every earlier step, on a grid longer than 512
  points** (`Volterra`'s reference branch and any history family above that
  length): the step sums
  `(t_i - t_j)^(H - 1/2) z_j` over all `j < i`, which is work that grows with
  the step index. The Markov lift is the answer where it applies — the whole
  `Rl*` family and `Volterra`'s rough kernels ride it — and the history block
  runs the direct convolution itself, exactly as the host does, for grids up
  to 512 points; that is how `RoughHeston`, `FBatesSvj`, `RoughBergomi` and
  `Lfsm` reach a device. Longer grids stay on the host, which the process's
  `device_ready()` decides.
- **A variance with less than one degree of freedom** (`Svcgmy` at
  `4κη/ζ² < 1`): the exact square-root step then draws a Poisson mixture the
  kernels do not carry, and the process stays on the host; at one degree or
  more the step is the square of a shifted normal plus a central χ², which the
  frame's gamma draw supplies, and the series block sizes the terms live.
- **A runtime dimension that couples its rows, above four** (`Wishart`, and
  `Lmm` / `MultiGbm` / `Mcgns` above four): the rows share a drift or a
  correlated noise, so they cannot be stepped as independent launches the way
  `Bgm`, `Adg` and `WuZhangD` are, and a launch carries four slots and four
  shocks; up to four they ride one launch, above it they stay on the host
  whatever the backend.
- **More time-varying coefficients than the eight the kernel binds**: none
  today. `MultifactorSabr` needed three, `Adg` five and `Hjm` six, and all
  three now declare them — the first turned a per-step bucket search into a
  buffer read, the last seven time-only callables into six tabulated curves.
- **A coefficient that is a Rust closure of the state** (`Cheyette`'s
  `sigma(t, x)`, `VolterraSde`'s drift and diffusion): a closure of time alone
  is tabulated as a curve the way `Cir2F`'s `phi(t)` is; one that reads the
  state has no representation a kernel can evaluate. See below.
- **Point processes on their own clock** (`MultivariateHawkes`, and `Hawkes`
  in its horizon mode): the output length is the number of events, not the
  grid. `Hawkes` in count mode is one event per step and reaches the engine
  through the exact exponential-excitation recursion; `Ctrw` is a grid process
  and reaches it under exponential waits, while its gamma, inverse-Gaussian
  and stable waits have no per-cell Poisson count and stay on the host.
  `Poisson`, `CompoundPoisson`, `CustomJt` and `CompoundCustom` split here —
  their count mode is a fixed-length running sum and reaches the engine,
  their horizon mode does not.
- **A per-step search over a table finer than 512 points**
  (`InverseAlphaStableSubordinator` with more `u_steps` than that): the table
  block keeps the direct subordinator in a per-path array of 512 values and
  scans it; a finer table stays on the host.
- **More regimes than the four the chain family carries**
  (`RegimeSwitchingDiffusion` at five or more): the family holds four
  volatilities and four rows of thresholds; larger chains stay on the host
  whatever the backend.
- **A lag order above one** (`ARp`, `MAq` or the GARCH family built above
  order one): the recursion reads back as many past values as the order and a
  step holds four slots. At order one they reach a device; above it the
  process says so rather than falling back silently. `Arima` and `Sarima` are
  the exception: a linear recursion of white noise is a moving-average filter,
  so the history block runs them at any order as the convolution of the
  innovations with the recursion's own impulse response.
- **A two-dimensional sheet rather than a path** (`Fbs`): its output is an
  `m x n` field from a two-dimensional FFT.
- **More variance factors than a launch's four shocks carry**
  (`MultifactorHeston` at `K >= 3`): each factor takes two shocks, so one
  and two factors ride the double-Heston family and three or more stay on the
  host whatever the backend.

### The last five, and how each would reach a device

Five processes have no device route today. None is ruled out for good; each
names the one facility the engine still lacks, and the facilities are of the
kind the lift, history, series and table blocks already are — a clause in the
family table plus a block in the frame, never a hand-written kernel.

- **`Wishart`** at `d ≤ 2` is a transcription rather than a new facility: the
  Ahdida–Alfonsi step is a basis change by three `2 × 2` maps folded on the
  host, then per coordinate a squared Bessel draw — a non-central χ² with a
  state-dependent non-centrality, which at one degree of freedom or more is
  the square of a shifted normal plus a central χ², the form `Svcgmy`'s
  variance already takes on the engine — and Gaussian updates of the
  off-diagonal entry; at `d = 2` the extended Cholesky of the remaining
  `1 × 1` block is a square root. What is left to decide is the rank-adaptive
  branch the host takes when that block degenerates, and the Poisson mixture
  below one degree of freedom, neither of which a family can express today.
  `d ≥ 3` stays on the host for want of slots.
- **`MultivariateHawkes`** has a ragged output — one event list per
  dimension over a horizon — and no count mode. A device version is an API
  addition, not a port: a count mode that produces a fixed number of events
  as two rows, time and mark, and a family with the state `(t, λ_1, …, λ_3)`
  for `d ≤ 3`. With a common decay the exact recursion that runs `Hawkes`
  generalises; with the per-pair decays the process allows, the next event
  needs thinning, a random number of proposals a step — a `thinning { rate
  (..) bound (..) }` clause drawing hashed uniforms per attempt, as the
  frame's Poisson count already does.
- **`Fbs`** is a two-dimensional circulant embedding: the eigenvalues of the
  `2m × 2n` covariance block by a two-dimensional FFT once, then per sample a
  two-dimensional FFT of complex Gaussian noise scaled by their square roots.
  That is a device *pipeline* beside the fGN one, not a family: row FFTs, a
  transpose, column FFTs on each backend's existing batched FFT (cuFFT has
  native two-dimensional plans), about a day per backend.
- **`Cheyette` and `VolterraSde`** hold Rust closures of the state. Three
  routes, in order of preference: a **coefficient expression tree** the user
  builds instead of a closure (`Expr::state().sqrt() * Expr::param(0) + …`),
  compiled on the host to a short stack bytecode and interpreted in the kernel
  from a per-launch instruction buffer — exact and general, the frame gaining
  one bounded evaluation loop; **tabulation** of the closure on a `(t, x)` grid
  with bilinear interpolation in the kernel — approximate, but no change for
  the caller, and good for smooth coefficients; or a **closed set of shapes**
  (`DeviceFn`: constant, affine, power, exponential, spline in time) mapped to
  a family each — cheap and restrictive. The expression tree is the durable
  answer, the tabulation the stopgap.

## What a family is

A process reaches a device by declaring which of the engine's **116 families**
it steps, not by writing a kernel. A family is one entry in
`stochastic-rs-stochastic/src/euler/families.rs` — its parameters, its state
components, its noise components, one step expression per component and one
report expression — and the same tokens generate the host step, the C body
the CUDA and Metal kernels render, and the CubeCL functions. Adding a process
is a declaration plus an `EulerCoefficients` (one component) or `EulerSystem`
(more than one) impl; no kernel is edited.

The engine's kernels carry four state slots, four independent noise
components, twenty scalar parameters, two uniforms, a Poisson jump count,
the sum of that step's jump sizes — under a normal, double-exponential,
tempered-stable, Rademacher or symmetric-stable law, the first two also
compounded as `∏(1 + y) − 1` for a multiplicative jump or taken one per step
for an event-indexed path — and up to two Gamma draws. A family with a `lift` clause runs under the Markov lift of a
rough Volterra kernel: the frame keeps up to 176 nodes' two state vectors per
path, evaluates the family's drift, diffusion and shock before the step and
hands it the lifted value as `lv`. A family with a `history` clause keeps, per
path, the values it pushes each step — up to 512 grid points — and reads their
convolution with a weight curve the launch tabulates, indexed by lag, as
`cv`: the exact O(n²) scheme of a moving average over the whole past, which is
a Volterra integral on the grid, an ARMA filter or a fractional stable motion
depending on the weights. A family with a `series` clause has the frame draw,
per path and before the steps, as many shot-noise terms as the launch names —
a unit-rate arrival, an exponential, two uniforms and an arrival time each —
size them with the family's own expression and sum them into the grid cells
their times fall in, up to 512; the step reads its cell as `sj`. A family with a
`table` clause has the frame build, per path, a monotone table of up to 512
values from its increment expression, doubling the extent until the table
reaches the horizon, and hands each step the interpolated inverse at its time
as `iv`. A launch binds up to eight time-varying coefficients, which
a family names `ct` and `ct1` through `ct7`; it pays one buffer read per
declared curve per step, so a model with one still pays for one. The first
two noise components may come from a fractional pipeline instead of the
kernel's own hash stream: one embedding produces both when the rows share a
Hurst exponent, which is what a correlated fractional pair needs. A
process whose first grid point is itself a draw — a conditional-variance
model's series, a noise generator — asks the launch to step before writing
it. A model that wants correlated shocks draws the components independently
and correlates them in its own step, which is what the host samplers do.

Feature flags: `metal` (`Metal`), `cuda` (`Cuda`),
`cubecl-cuda` and `cubecl-wgpu`, the two runtimes a `Cubecl` handle can name
(`Cubecl::cuda(0)`, `Cubecl::wgpu(0)` — wgpu is Metal on macOS, Vulkan on
Linux, WebGPU on the web), `accelerate`. Both runtimes can be compiled into
one build and used at once. A marker only exists when its feature is
compiled, so selecting a backend a process does not support — or one the
build does not carry — is a compile error, never a silent fallback. The same
holds for precision: the capability traits take the scalar
(`FgnBackend<T>`, `EulerBackend<T>`) and a device implements them only for
the precision its kernels compute in, so `Gbm<f64>` or `Fgn<f64>` on
`Metal` or `Cubecl` does not compile. Nothing is computed in `f32`
behind an `f64` type.

## What the device path does

- **fGN noise.** The circulant-embedding FFT that produces fractional
  Gaussian noise runs on the device, one launch per `sample_par` batch.
  `Fgn` returns it directly.
- **Fractional diffusions.** `Fbm`, `Fou`, `Fcir`, `Fgbm`, `FJacobi`,
  `FVasicek`, `Cfgns`, `Cfbms` and `Cfou` hand
  those increments to the Euler engine, which runs the whole recursion in the
  kernel. They declare no kernel of their own: `Fou` is the same
  Ornstein–Uhlenbeck family a Gaussian `Ou` uses, `Fgbm` the same geometric
  one, and a step that multiplies by an increment does not care whether the
  increment was hashed or produced by an FFT. The two kernels meet in device
  memory: the pipeline hands its own buffer to the Euler launch instead of
  reading the increments back, so a batch crosses the bus once, for the paths.
  Measured on an M-series Metal device against the host: fOU with 2000 paths
  of 512 steps went from 101 ms to 5.8 ms, fBM with 4000 paths of 1024 steps
  from 252 ms to 19.2 ms. Keeping the increments on the device is most of
  that: through host memory the same runs took 25 ms and 93 ms.
- **Euler engine.** The whole Euler–Maruyama recursion of a path runs in one
  kernel thread; `sample_par(m)` is one launch for all `m` paths, `sample()`
  launches a single path. A system writes one plane per component, so a
  Heston draw comes back as the two arrays it always did. CIR uses full
  truncation (Lord, Koekkoek & van Dijk 2010) in the kernel. The CUDA and
  Metal kernels are one C body rendered per language, and the CubeCL kernel
  repeats its integer hash, so `Metal`, `Cuda` and `Cubecl` agree with each
  other seed for seed up to `f32` libm rounding — which
  `euler::family_parity` checks by launching every declared family on both
  kernels and comparing them point for point.
- **Host backends.** `Cpu` is the process's own sampler; `Accelerate` is a
  CPU device that only differs for the fGN family (vDSP FFT). For every
  other process both are the same code path.

## Large batches

A device handle holds at most `batch_budget` bytes of path data per launch
(1 GiB by default, `STOCHASTIC_RS_DEVICE_BATCH_BYTES`, or
`Cuda::default().with_batch_budget(n)`). A larger `sample_par(m)` runs as
several launches whose union is bit-identical to one launch, because the kernels
hash the global path index; `sample_map(m, f)` maps each chunk in parallel on
the host before the next chunk is launched, so the batch never has to fit in
memory at once. On native CUDA the chunks alternate between two streams: one
copies back through pinned memory while the next computes. The fGN devices chunk
the same way, one seed per batch and an element offset per chunk, and keep the
FFT plans and buffers of the last four `(n, m)` shapes, so alternating two sizes
does not re-plan on every call.

## More than one GPU

The handle names the device: `Cuda::new(1)` (CUDA ordinal),
`Metal::new(1)` (index into `Device::all()`), `Cubecl::cuda(1)` /
`Cubecl::wgpu(1)` (the runtime and its device index); `.on::<B>()` puts a process on it, and
`handle.probe()` reports the device opened. The defaults read
`STOCHASTIC_RS_DEVICE`. From Python the name carries the ordinal:
`device="cuda:1"`, `probe_device("metal:0")`.

## When the device is missing

`handle.probe()` on any backend handle returns `Ok(DeviceInfo)` with the device's name and
precisions, or `Err(DeviceError)` saying why it cannot be used (`Unavailable`,
`Compile`, `Launch`). Every process has `try_sample()` and `try_sample_par(m)`
on `ProcessExt`, returning the same error instead of panicking (always `Ok` on
the host devices, bit-identical to `sample*`); the plain `sample*` calls panic
with the error's message and never fall back to the host. From Python a device
failure is a `RuntimeError`.

## Precision and reproducibility

| Backend | Precision | Same seed, same device | Bit-identical to `Cpu` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cpu` | `f32` / `f64` | yes, thread-count independent | — |
| `Accelerate` | `f32` / `f64` | reproducible-effort only (vDSP reduction order can differ across core types) | no |
| `Metal` | `f32` (an `f64` process is a compile error) | yes | no |
| `Cuda` | `f32` / `f64` (a failing `f64` launch is reported, never replaced by the `f32` kernel) | yes | no |
| `Cubecl` | `f32` (an `f64` process is a compile error) | yes | no |

The device seed is drawn from the process's own seed source, so the device
follows the host's rule exactly: two processes built from the same
`Deterministic` seed value give the same paths, consecutive calls on one
process advance the stream into independent paths, and an `Unseeded` process
draws fresh entropy every call. Host and device
paths agree in distribution, not bit for bit, because the device draws its
own stream.

## From Python

The device-capable classes take `device=` (`PyGbm(..., dtype="f32", device="metal")`;
the fGN family and the Euler engine's three processes), checked and probed at
construction. `PyGbm(0.05, 0.2, 253, x0=100.0, t=1.0, seed=7, device="metal", dtype="f32").sample_par(m)` runs the Euler
engine with `device` one of `"cpu"`, `"accelerate"`, `"cuda"`, `"metal"`,
`"cubecl-cuda"`, `"cubecl-wgpu"` — every name is one concrete backend, there
is no "pick whatever is compiled" name; one the build does not carry raises
`ValueError` with a rebuild hint. The array is `float64` from
the CPU and native CUDA paths and `float32` from Metal and CubeCL, whose
kernels compute in single precision. The published wheels are
CPU-only; the device backends come with a source build —
`maturin develop --features metal,cubecl-wgpu` on a Mac,
`--features cuda` on an NVIDIA machine.

## Adding a kernel

A process joins the Euler engine by implementing `EulerCoefficients`
(its family, initial state, grid, horizon and device seed, plus the curve if
it reads one) and switching its `backend_switch!` line from `via host` to
`via euler`. A new family is one entry in `euler/families.rs` plus one
`EulerSpec` variant and one dispatch line in the hand-written CubeCL kernel;
the C body the CUDA and Metal kernels render is generated from the same
declaration. Widening a process's bound from `HostBackend` to `EulerBackend`
is not a breaking change for its callers.

`Cfou` reaches a device only halfway: its fGN pair is drawn there, through
the same pipeline `Fgn` uses, and the complex recursion that consumes it runs
on the host. Closing that gap needs a second increment buffer, since the
kernel reads one.

`JumpFou`, `JumpFOUCustom` and `Sde` carry a backend parameter that changes
nothing: the two jump processes fill their fGN on the host because moving it
would change their pinned streams, and `Sde` drives its own loop over
caller-supplied closures. Their `.on::<B>()` compiles and does nothing, which
is a wart to close rather than a feature.

What each remaining group would take: a series representation needs a sort
