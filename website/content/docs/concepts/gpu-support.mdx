Every process carries its sampling backend as a type parameter and is re-typed with `.on::<B>()` (see [backends](/docs/concepts/backends)). This page is the inventory: which processes run on which device, at what precision, and which configurations stay on the host.

## The support matrix

| Family | Processes | Capability bound | `Cpu` | `Accelerate` | `Metal` | `Cuda` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fGN noise | `Fgn` (1) | `FgnBackend<T>` | SIMD + `ndrustfft` | vDSP FFT | MSL FFT pipeline, `f32` only | cuFFT + Philox, `f32` / `f64` |
| Sheet | `Fbs` (1) | `SheetBackend<T>` | SIMD + `ndrustfft` | same as `Cpu` | MSL sheet pipeline, `f32` only | cuFFT + Philox, `f32` / `f64` |
| Euler engine, one component | 91 processes | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | Euler–Maruyama kernel in MSL, `f32` only | NVRTC kernel, `f32` / `f64` |
| Euler engine, systems | 39 processes | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | the same kernel, up to four components | the same kernel |
| Euler engine, fractional | `Fbm`, `Fou`, `Fcir`, `Fgbm`, `FJacobi`, `FVasicek`, `Cfgns`, `Cfbms`, `Cfou`, `JumpFou`, `JumpFOUCustom` (11, counted above) | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | fGN pipeline feeds the same MSL kernel | fGN pipeline feeds the same NVRTC kernel |

Every process reaches a device: `Fgn` and `Fbs` through pipelines of their own, the other 130 through the Euler engine. The engine covers the diffusions; the stochastic-volatility models under both their Euler and quadratic-exponential schemes; the short-rate models, including the curve-driven ones; the fractional processes; the jump models (a process generic over its jump-size law when that law is `SimdNormal` or `SimdExp`); the Lévy processes and subordinators; the order-(1,1) conditional-variance and first-order lag models; the noise generators; the Riemann–Liouville and Volterra models under the Markov lift of their kernel; the rough-volatility models with a memory integral, `Lfsm`, `Arima` and `Sarima` on the host's exact scheme for grids up to 512 points; the point processes in count mode; and the matrix and basket models within the limits below.

## What stays on the host

The engine steps a bounded forward recursion over four scalar state slots and four shocks. These configurations exceed it and sample on the host, bit-identically to the `Cpu` build, whatever the backend; `device_fallback()` names the reason:

- **History models on a long grid.** `RoughHeston`, `FBatesSvj`, `RoughBergomi`, `Lfsm`, `Arima`, `Sarima` and `Volterra`'s reference-convolution branch read their whole past each step, which the kernels carry for grids up to 512 points.
- **Coefficients written as a Rust closure, a Python callable or a `Grid2D`** (`Cheyette`'s `sigma(t, x)`, `VolterraSde`'s drift and diffusion, `HestonSlv`'s leverage). Written as an `Expr`, the same coefficient runs on the device — see below.
- **Too many coupled dimensions.** `Wishart` above `d = 2` or below degree 3; `Lmm`, `MultiGbm` and `Mcgns` above four components; `MultifactorHeston` with three or more factors; `RegimeSwitchingDiffusion` with five or more regimes; `MultivariateHawkes` above two components or with per-pair decays.
- **Point processes on their own clock.** `Hawkes`, `MultivariateHawkes`, `Poisson`, `CompoundPoisson`, `CustomJt` and `CompoundCustom` in horizon mode, where the output length is the number of events; `CustomJt` / `CompoundCustom` with an inter-arrival law other than the exponential; `Ctrw` with gamma, inverse-Gaussian or stable waits.
- **Jump-size laws other than `SimdNormal` / `SimdExp`** for the processes generic over one (`Merton`, `Kou`, `LevyDiffusion`, `Bates1996`, `JumpFou`, `JumpFOUCustom`).
- **Lag order above one** for `ARp`, `MAq` and the GARCH family.
- **`Svcgmy`** with `4κη/ζ² < 1`, where the exact variance step needs a Poisson mixture.
- **`InverseAlphaStableSubordinator`** with more than 512 `u_steps`.
- **`Fbs`** when `m − 1` or `n − 1` is not a power of two.

### Coefficients as expressions

`Cheyette`, `VolterraSde` and `HestonSlv` take their coefficients as `Fn2D` callables of time and state. A Rust closure or a Python callable runs on the host only. Written as an `Expr`, the same coefficient reaches the device:

```rust
use stochastic_rs::traits::Expr;

// σ(t, x) = 0.01 + 0.4 |x|, a displaced local volatility
let sigma = (Expr::lit(0.01) + Expr::x().abs() * 0.4).compile()?;
let cheyette = Cheyette::<f32, _>::new(f0, 0.5, sigma, 252, Some(1.0), seed).on::<Cuda>();
```

`Expr::t()` and `Expr::x()` are the time and the state, `Expr::lit` a constant; the arithmetic operators, `sqrt`, `exp`, `ln`, `abs`, `tanh`, `powf`, `max` and `min` build the tree. `Expr::compile()` (or `Fn2D::try_from`) turns it into a postfix `Program` of at most 62 operations on a stack of at most 8, and returns a `ProgramError` for a tree no kernel could run; `HestonSlv::new(.., leverage.compile()?, ..)` is the shape. The host evaluates the same program, so host and device compute the same coefficient, not a tabulation of it.

## What the device path does

- **fGN noise.** The circulant-embedding FFT runs on the device, one launch per `sample_par` batch.
- **Sheets.** `Fbs` runs its two-dimensional circulant embedding as a pipeline beside the fGN one; the embedding's eigenvalue roots are computed once on the host when the process is built.
- **Fractional diffusions.** `Fbm`, `Fou`, `Fcir`, `Fgbm`, `FJacobi`, `FVasicek`, `Cfgns`, `Cfbms` and `Cfou` take their increments from the fGN pipeline in device memory and run the whole recursion in the Euler kernel, so a batch crosses the bus once. On an M-series Metal device against the host: fOU with 2000 paths of 512 steps took 5.8 ms against 101 ms, fBM with 4000 paths of 1024 steps 19.2 ms against 252 ms.
- **Euler engine.** The whole Euler–Maruyama recursion of a path runs in one kernel thread; `sample_par(m)` is one launch for all `m` paths. A system writes one plane per component, so a Heston draw comes back as two arrays. CIR uses full truncation (Lord, Koekkoek & van Dijk 2010) in the kernel. The CUDA and Metal kernels are one C body rendered per language, so `Metal` and `Cuda` agree seed for seed up to `f32` libm rounding.
- **Host backends.** `Cpu` is the process's own sampler; `Accelerate` differs from it only for the fGN family (vDSP FFT).

## Large batches

A device handle holds at most `batch_budget` bytes of path data per launch (1 GiB by default; `Cuda::from_env()?` reads `STOCHASTIC_RS_DEVICE_BATCH_BYTES`, or set `Cuda::default().with_batch_budget(n)`). A larger `sample_par(m)` runs as several launches whose union is bit-identical to one launch, because the kernels hash the global path index; `sample_map(m, f)` maps each chunk on the host before the next is launched, so the batch never has to fit in memory at once. The fGN devices chunk the same way.

## Precision and reproducibility

| Backend | Precision | Same seed, same device | Bit-identical to `Cpu` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cpu` | `f32` / `f64` | yes, thread-count independent | — |
| `Accelerate` | `f32` / `f64` | reproducible-effort only (vDSP reduction order can differ across core types) | no |
| `Metal` | `f32` (an `f64` process is a compile error) | yes | no |
| `Cuda` | `f32` / `f64` (a failing `f64` launch is reported, never replaced by the `f32` kernel) | yes | no |

The device seed is drawn from the process's own seed source, so the device follows the host's rule: two processes built from the same `Deterministic` seed value give the same paths, consecutive calls on one process advance the stream, and an `Unseeded` process draws fresh entropy every call. Host and device paths agree in distribution, not bit for bit, because the device draws its own stream.

Probing a device, handling its failures and choosing an ordinal are on [Sampling backends](/docs/concepts/backends).

## From Python

89 process classes take `device=` (`PyGbm(..., dtype="f32", device="metal")`); a class without it samples on the host. The name is one of `"cpu"`, `"accelerate"`, `"cuda"`, `"metal"`, optionally with an ordinal (`"cuda:1"`); one the build does not carry raises `ValueError` with a rebuild hint, and the device is probed when the object is built. Arrays come back in the class's dtype, and the `f32`-only Metal backend needs `dtype="f32"`. A class also answers `device_ready()` and `device_fallback()` for the configuration it holds. The published wheels are CPU-only; the device backends need a source build — `maturin develop --features metal` on a Mac, `--features cuda` on an NVIDIA machine. See [Python bindings](/docs/python#devices).

## Trying the CUDA back-end without a CUDA machine

[`notebooks/colab_cuda_check.ipynb`](https://colab.research.google.com/github/rust-dd/stochastic-rs/blob/main/notebooks/colab_cuda_check.ipynb) opens in Google Colab: on a T4 runtime it installs Rust, clones the repository, runs the native CUDA tests (the fGN pipeline and the Euler engine), builds the Python wheel with `device="cuda"` and plots what the GPU produced. The Rust tests build in 10–20 minutes on Colab's two cores, the wheel in another ten.

### A run on a T4

[`notebooks/runs/colab_cuda_check_T4_2026-09-03.ipynb`](https://github.com/rust-dd/stochastic-rs/blob/main/notebooks/runs/colab_cuda_check_T4_2026-09-03.ipynb) is the executed copy of a run on 2026-09-03 (Tesla T4, driver 580.82 / CUDA 13.0, toolkit 12.8; GitHub's viewer may not render Colab outputs, [nbviewer](https://nbviewer.org/github/rust-dd/stochastic-rs/blob/main/notebooks/runs/colab_cuda_check_T4_2026-09-03.ipynb) does). ## Stability

What is public and stable is the capability a bound names (`EulerBackend<T>` and the others), `.on::<B>()`, `with_backend(handle)`, `device_ready()`, `device_fallback()`, `probe()` and the `try_*` calls. The engine's launch hooks, family specifications and slot limits are hidden from the rendered documentation and outside the stability promise.
