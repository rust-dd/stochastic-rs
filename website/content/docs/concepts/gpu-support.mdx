Every process carries its sampling backend as a type parameter and is
re-typed with `.on::<B>()` (see [backends](/docs/concepts/backends)). This
page is the inventory: which processes have a device kernel today, on which
backends, and what the rest do.

## The support matrix

| Family | Processes | Capability bound | `Cpu` | `Accelerate` | `Metal` | `Cuda` | `Cubecl` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fGN noise | `Fgn` (1) | `FgnBackend<T>` | SIMD + `ndrustfft` | vDSP FFT | MSL FFT pipeline, `f32` only | cuFFT + Philox, `f32` / `f64` | CubeCL FFT, `f32` only |
| Euler engine, one component | 68 processes | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | Euler–Maruyama kernel in MSL, `f32` only | NVRTC kernel, `f32` / `f64` | CubeCL kernel, `f32` only |
| Euler engine, systems | 22 processes | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | the same kernel, up to four components | the same kernel | the same kernel |
| Euler engine, fractional | `Fbm`, `Fou`, `Fcir`, `Fgbm`, `FJacobi`, `FVasicek`, `Cfgns`, `Cfbms`, `Cfou` (9, counted above) | `EulerBackend<T>` | the process's own SIMD sampler | same as `Cpu` | fGN pipeline feeds the same MSL kernel | fGN pipeline feeds the same NVRTC kernel | fGN pipeline feeds the same CubeCL kernel |
| Host only | the remaining 28 processes | `HostBackend` | own SIMD sampler | compile error | compile error | compile error | compile error |

The 90 processes on the engine cover the diffusions, the stochastic-volatility
models under both their Euler and quadratic-exponential schemes (`Heston`,
`HestonLog`, `DoubleHeston`, `Heston2D`, `Sabr`, `Bergomi`,
`HestonStochCorr`, `BatesSvj`, `Hkde`), the short-rate models including the
curve-driven ones (`HullWhite`, `HullWhite2F`, `HoLee`, `BlackKarasinski`,
`CirPlusPlus`, `Cir2F`, `DuffieKan`, `DuffieKanJumpExp`, `Vasicek`,
`FVasicek`), the fractional processes, the jump models (`MjdLog`, `BatesSvj`,
`Hkde`, `HawkesJD`, `DuffieKanJumpExp`), the Lévy processes whose increment
is one draw with no rejection (`Ig`, `Nig`, `IGSubordinator`,
`PoissonSubordinator`, `AlphaStableSubordinator`, `GammaSubordinator`,
`TemperedStableSubordinator`, `Vg`, `BilateralGamma`, `BilateralGammaMotion`,
`Bns`), the discrete-time conditional-variance models at order (1,1)
(`Arch`, `Garch`, `GjrGarch`, `Agarch`, `Egarch`), the first-order lag models
(`ARp`, `MAq`), the noise generators (`Wn`, `Gn`, `Cgns`, `Cfgns`), the
correlated fractional pair (`Cfbms`, `Cfou`), the bounded correlation
processes (`TengSCP`, `VanEmmerich`, `TransformedOU`), the two models whose
coefficients travel as curves rather than parameters (`MultifactorSabr`'s
term structure, `Hjm`'s six time-dependent coefficients), the matrix-valued
models whose rows are independent copies of one small family (`Bgm`'s forward
rates, `Adg`'s affine factors, `WuZhangD`'s rate–variance pairs — one launch
per row) and `Poisson` in its count mode.

What stays on the host, and why. The engine steps a bounded forward
recursion over four scalar state slots, so what rules a process out is
unbounded work per step, unbounded state, or an output that is not a fixed
grid:

- **A history integral over every earlier step** (`RoughHeston`,
  `FBatesSvj`, `RoughBergomi`, `Lfsm`, `Volterra` in its direct branch): the
  step sums `(t_i - t_j)^(H - 1/2) z_j` over all `j < i`, which is work that
  grows with the step index.
- **Markov-lift schemes with a runtime-sized state** (`RlBlackScholes`,
  `RlFBm`, `RlFOU`, `RlHeston`, `Volterra` in its lift branch): the lift
  exists precisely to avoid that convolution, but it carries roughly
  `2 (ln n + 20)` state values, where the engine carries four.
- **Series representations over the whole horizon** (`Cgmy`, `Cts`, `KoBoL`,
  `Rdts`, `Svcgmy`): the path is built by drawing a fixed number of jumps for
  the whole horizon, sorting them by arrival time and accumulating onto the
  grid — a global construction with a sort, not a step recursion.
- **A runtime dimension that couples its rows** (`Lmm`, `MultiGbm`, `Mcgns`,
  `Wishart`): the rows share a drift or a correlated noise, so they cannot be
  stepped as independent launches the way `Bgm`, `Adg` and `WuZhangD` are.: the component count is a runtime
  value, or a parameter is an `Array1` / `Array2` rather than a fixed set of
  scalars.
- **More time-varying coefficients than the eight the kernel binds**: none
  today. `MultifactorSabr` needed three, `Adg` five and `Hjm` six, and all
  three now declare them — the first turned a per-step bucket search into a
  buffer read, the last seven time-only callables into six tabulated curves.
- **A coefficient that reads the state** (`Cheyette`): its `sigma(t, x)` is a
  user closure of time *and* the state, so it cannot be tabulated as a curve
  the way `Cir2F`'s `phi(t)` is. A closure of time alone is not a blocker.
- **Point processes on their own clock** (`Hawkes`, `MultivariateHawkes`,
  `Ctrw`): the output length is the number of events, not the grid.
  `Poisson` splits here — its count mode is a fixed-length running sum and
  reaches the engine, its horizon mode does not.
- **A per-step inversion or search** (`InverseAlphaStableSubordinator`): the
  step scans an auxiliary path rather than drawing a value.
- **A matrix exponential and a runtime regime count**
  (`RegimeSwitchingDiffusion`).
- **Differencing with a runtime lag order** (`Arima`, `Sarima`, and any of
  `ARp`, `MAq` or the GARCH family built above order one): the recursion
  reads back as many past values as the order. At order one they all reach a
  device; above it the process says so rather than falling back silently.
- **A two-dimensional sheet rather than a path** (`Fbs`): its output is an
  `m x n` field from a two-dimensional FFT.
- **A tuple output at a compile-time factor count** (`MultifactorHeston`):
  at `K = 1` and `K = 2` its step is exactly the existing `Heston` and
  `DoubleHeston` families, but the launch would need `D = K + 1` components,
  which is a const expression the language does not yet let a generic impl
  form. `K >= 3` needs `2K` noise components, past the four the kernel
  carries.

## What a family is

A process reaches a device by declaring which of the engine's **91 families**
it steps, not by writing a kernel. A family is one entry in
`stochastic-rs-stochastic/src/euler/families.rs` — its parameters, its state
components, its noise components, one step expression per component and one
report expression — and the same tokens generate the host step, the C body
the CUDA and Metal kernels render, and the CubeCL functions. Adding a process
is a declaration plus an `EulerCoefficients` (one component) or `EulerSystem`
(more than one) impl; no kernel is edited.

The engine's kernels carry four state slots, four independent noise
components, twenty scalar parameters, one time-varying coefficient, two
uniforms, a Poisson jump count, the sum of that step's jump sizes and up to
two Gamma draws. A launch binds up to eight time-varying coefficients, which
a family names `ct` and `ct1` through `ct7`; it pays one buffer read per
declared curve per step, so a model with one still pays for one. The first
two noise components may come from a fractional pipeline instead of the
kernel's own hash stream: one embedding produces both when the rows share a
Hurst exponent, which is what a correlated fractional pair needs. A
process whose first grid point is itself a draw — a conditional-variance
model's series, a noise generator — asks the launch to step before writing
it. A model that wants correlated shocks draws the components independently
and correlates them in its own step, which is what the host samplers do.

Feature flags: `metal` (`Metal`), `cuda` (`Cuda`),
`cubecl-cuda` and `cubecl-wgpu`, the two runtimes a `Cubecl` handle can name
(`Cubecl::cuda(0)`, `Cubecl::wgpu(0)` — wgpu is Metal on macOS, Vulkan on
Linux, WebGPU on the web), `accelerate`. Both runtimes can be compiled into
one build and used at once. A marker only exists when its feature is
compiled, so selecting a backend a process does not support — or one the
build does not carry — is a compile error, never a silent fallback. The same
holds for precision: the capability traits take the scalar
(`FgnBackend<T>`, `EulerBackend<T>`) and a device implements them only for
the precision its kernels compute in, so `Gbm<f64>` or `Fgn<f64>` on
`Metal` or `Cubecl` does not compile. Nothing is computed in `f32`
behind an `f64` type.

## What the device path does

- **fGN noise.** The circulant-embedding FFT that produces fractional
  Gaussian noise runs on the device, one launch per `sample_par` batch.
  `Fgn` returns it directly.
- **Fractional diffusions.** `Fbm`, `Fou`, `Fcir`, `Fgbm`, `FJacobi`,
  `FVasicek`, `Cfgns`, `Cfbms` and `Cfou` hand
  those increments to the Euler engine, which runs the whole recursion in the
  kernel. They declare no kernel of their own: `Fou` is the same
  Ornstein–Uhlenbeck family a Gaussian `Ou` uses, `Fgbm` the same geometric
  one, and a step that multiplies by an increment does not care whether the
  increment was hashed or produced by an FFT. The two kernels meet in device
  memory: the pipeline hands its own buffer to the Euler launch instead of
  reading the increments back, so a batch crosses the bus once, for the paths.
  Measured on an M-series Metal device against the host: fOU with 2000 paths
  of 512 steps went from 101 ms to 5.8 ms, fBM with 4000 paths of 1024 steps
  from 252 ms to 19.2 ms. Keeping the increments on the device is most of
  that: through host memory the same runs took 25 ms and 93 ms.
- **Euler engine.** The whole Euler–Maruyama recursion of a path runs in one
  kernel thread; `sample_par(m)` is one launch for all `m` paths, `sample()`
  launches a single path. A system writes one plane per component, so a
  Heston draw comes back as the two arrays it always did. CIR uses full
  truncation (Lord, Koekkoek & van Dijk 2010) in the kernel. The CUDA and
  Metal kernels are one C body rendered per language, and the CubeCL kernel
  repeats its integer hash, so `Metal`, `Cuda` and `Cubecl` agree with each
  other seed for seed up to `f32` libm rounding — which
  `euler::family_parity` checks by launching every declared family on both
  kernels and comparing them point for point.
- **Host backends.** `Cpu` is the process's own sampler; `Accelerate` is a
  CPU device that only differs for the fGN family (vDSP FFT). For every
  other process both are the same code path.

## Large batches

A device handle holds at most `batch_budget` bytes of path data per launch
(1 GiB by default, `STOCHASTIC_RS_DEVICE_BATCH_BYTES`, or
`Cuda::default().with_batch_budget(n)`). A larger `sample_par(m)` runs as
several launches whose union is bit-identical to one launch, because the kernels
hash the global path index; `sample_map(m, f)` maps each chunk in parallel on
the host before the next chunk is launched, so the batch never has to fit in
memory at once. On native CUDA the chunks alternate between two streams: one
copies back through pinned memory while the next computes. The fGN devices chunk
the same way, one seed per batch and an element offset per chunk, and keep the
FFT plans and buffers of the last four `(n, m)` shapes, so alternating two sizes
does not re-plan on every call.

## More than one GPU

The handle names the device: `Cuda::new(1)` (CUDA ordinal),
`Metal::new(1)` (index into `Device::all()`), `Cubecl::cuda(1)` /
`Cubecl::wgpu(1)` (the runtime and its device index); `.on::<B>()` puts a process on it, and
`handle.probe()` reports the device opened. The defaults read
`STOCHASTIC_RS_DEVICE`. From Python the name carries the ordinal:
`device="cuda:1"`, `probe_device("metal:0")`.

## When the device is missing

`handle.probe()` on any backend handle returns `Ok(DeviceInfo)` with the device's name and
precisions, or `Err(DeviceError)` saying why it cannot be used (`Unavailable`,
`Compile`, `Launch`). Every process has `try_sample()` and `try_sample_par(m)`
on `ProcessExt`, returning the same error instead of panicking (always `Ok` on
the host devices, bit-identical to `sample*`); the plain `sample*` calls panic
with the error's message and never fall back to the host. From Python a device
failure is a `RuntimeError`.

## Precision and reproducibility

| Backend | Precision | Same seed, same device | Bit-identical to `Cpu` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cpu` | `f32` / `f64` | yes, thread-count independent | — |
| `Accelerate` | `f32` / `f64` | reproducible-effort only (vDSP reduction order can differ across core types) | no |
| `Metal` | `f32` (an `f64` process is a compile error) | yes | no |
| `Cuda` | `f32` / `f64` (a failing `f64` launch is reported, never replaced by the `f32` kernel) | yes | no |
| `Cubecl` | `f32` (an `f64` process is a compile error) | yes | no |

The device seed is drawn from the process's own seed source, so the device
follows the host's rule exactly: two processes built from the same
`Deterministic` seed value give the same paths, consecutive calls on one
process advance the stream into independent paths, and an `Unseeded` process
draws fresh entropy every call. Host and device
paths agree in distribution, not bit for bit, because the device draws its
own stream.

## From Python

The device-capable classes take `device=` (`PyGbm(..., dtype="f32", device="metal")`;
the fGN family and the Euler engine's three processes), checked and probed at
construction. `PyGbm(0.05, 0.2, 253, x0=100.0, t=1.0, seed=7, device="metal", dtype="f32").sample_par(m)` runs the Euler
engine with `device` one of `"cpu"`, `"accelerate"`, `"cuda"`, `"metal"`,
`"cubecl-cuda"`, `"cubecl-wgpu"` — every name is one concrete backend, there
is no "pick whatever is compiled" name; one the build does not carry raises
`ValueError` with a rebuild hint. The array is `float64` from
the CPU and native CUDA paths and `float32` from Metal and CubeCL, whose
kernels compute in single precision. The published wheels are
CPU-only; the device backends come with a source build —
`maturin develop --features metal,cubecl-wgpu` on a Mac,
`--features cuda` on an NVIDIA machine.

The Euler case is slower on the T4 in `f64` because that card executes
double precision at 1/32 of its single-precision rate, and the 200 MB result
is copied back and re-laid as rows on the host; `dtype="f32"` is the setting
for the Euler engine on inference-class GPUs. The fGN pipeline is FFT-bound
and wins already at these sizes; the gap widens with `m`.

## Adding a kernel

A process joins the Euler engine by implementing `EulerCoefficients`
(its family, initial state, grid, horizon and device seed, plus the curve if
it reads one) and switching its `backend_switch!` line from `via host` to
`via euler`. A new family is one entry in `euler/families.rs` plus one
`EulerSpec` variant and one dispatch line in the hand-written CubeCL kernel;
the C body the CUDA and Metal kernels render is generated from the same
declaration. Widening a process's bound from `HostBackend` to `EulerBackend`
is not a breaking change for its callers.

`Cfou` reaches a device only halfway: its fGN pair is drawn there, through
the same pipeline `Fgn` uses, and the complex recursion that consumes it runs
on the host. Closing that gap needs a second increment buffer, since the
kernel reads one.

`JumpFou`, `JumpFOUCustom` and `Sde` carry a backend parameter that changes
nothing: the two jump processes fill their fGN on the host because moving it
would change their pinned streams, and `Sde` drives its own loop over
caller-supplied closures. Their `.on::<B>()` compiles and does nothing, which
is a wart to close rather than a feature.

What each remaining group would take: a series representation needs a sort
over the whole horizon; a lag model needs state proportional to its order rather than four
slots; the Volterra and rough family needs a separate `O(n·N')` kernel
because its step is a convolution; and a dynamic-dimension model needs the
component count to be a launch argument rather than a property of its
family.
