The `stochastic-rs-copulas` crate ships bivariate and multivariate copulas, correlation utilities and an empirical copula. Fallible operations return `Result<_, CopulaError>`.

## Bivariate (`BivariateExt`)

**15** copulas, one per variant of `bivariate::BivariateFamily`. "Archimedean" below means the type implements the trait's `generator()`:

| Copula        | Family              | Tail dependence                                    |
|---------------|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Amh           | Archimedean         | None, except $\lambda_L = 1/2$ at $\theta = 1$ (Clayton(1)) |
| Bb1           | Archimedean (two-parameter) | Both, asymmetric ($\lambda_L = 2^{-1/(\theta\delta)}$, $\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\delta}$) |
| Bb7           | Archimedean (two-parameter) | Both, asymmetric ($\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\theta}$, $\lambda_L = 2^{-1/\delta}$) |
| Clayton       | Archimedean         | Lower only ($\lambda_L = 2^{-1/\theta}$)             |
| Fgm           | Perturbation        | None (bounded perturbation of independence)         |
| Frank         | Archimedean         | None (symmetric)                                    |
| Galambos      | Extreme-value       | Upper only ($\lambda_U = 2^{-1/\theta}$)             |
| Gaussian      | Elliptical          | None, for any $\rho$                                 |
| Gumbel        | Archimedean         | Upper only ($\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\theta}$)          |
| HuslerReiss   | Extreme-value       | Upper only ($\lambda_U = 2(1-\Phi(1/\lambda))$)      |
| Independence  | Trivial             | None                                                 |
| Joe           | Archimedean         | Upper only ($\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\theta}$)          |
| MarshallOlkin | Singular (shared-shock) | Upper only ($\lambda_U = \min(\alpha,\beta)$); has a singular component on $u^\alpha = v^\beta$ |
| Plackett      | Constant odds ratio | None                                                 |
| TCopula       | Elliptical          | Both, symmetric ($\lambda_L = \lambda_U$)            |

## Choosing a copula

The one-parameter families are fit by computing Kendall's tau and inverting it to the family's own parameter; BB1 and BB7 by two-parameter maximum likelihood. Short version:

| Need | Reach for |
|---|---|
| Both positive and negative dependence | `Frank`, `Gaussian`, `TCopula`, `Plackett` — the only four spanning the full `(-1, 1)` range of τ |
| Only ever positive dependence, lower-tail | `Clayton` — the only one-parameter family here with lower-tail dependence across its range (`Amh` has it only at $\theta = 1$, where it is Clayton(1); BB1 and BB7 carry both tails) |
| Only ever positive dependence, upper-tail | `Galambos`, `HuslerReiss`, `Gumbel`, `Joe` — `Gumbel` and `Joe` share the same upper-tail formula, so tail dependence alone won't distinguish them |
| Symmetric tail dependence (both tails, equal) | `TCopula` — the only one |
| Weak dependence in either direction (τ within roughly ±0.2-0.3) | `Amh`, `Fgm` — both structurally cannot represent stronger dependence |
| Fast sampling at scale (millions of draws) | `Amh`, `Clayton`, `Fgm`, `Frank`, `Gaussian`, `Independence`, `MarshallOlkin`, `Plackett` or `TCopula` — the nine with a closed-form `percent_point`; `Gumbel`, `Joe`, `Galambos`, `HuslerReiss`, `Bb1` and `Bb7` invert numerically per draw |
| A non-exchangeable, shared-shock dependence structure | `MarshallOlkin::new(alpha, beta)` — the one family here that is not symmetric in its two arguments in general |

### Which Kendall's τ a family reaches

`tau_from_theta` and `theta_from_tau` convert between τ and the family's parameter, and `fit` inverts the sample τ-b through the second:

| Reachable τ | Families |
|---|---|
| Full `(-1, 1)` | `Frank`, `Gaussian`, `TCopula`, `Plackett` |
| `[0, 1)` | `Clayton`, `Galambos`, `HuslerReiss`, `Gumbel`, `Joe` |
| Narrow, both signs | `Amh` ($[(5 - 8\ln 2)/3,\ 1/3] \approx [-0.1817,\ 0.3333]$), `Fgm` ($[-2/9,\ 2/9]$) |
| `{0}` | `Independence`, whose `theta_from_tau` answers $\theta = 0$ for any τ |
| `(0, 1]` on the symmetric slice | `MarshallOlkin`: `from_tau` sets $\alpha = \beta = \theta$ and reaches $\tau = \theta/(2 - \theta)$; `new(alpha, beta)` sets the two shock rates apart and is the one family here that is not exchangeable in general |

A τ̂ the family cannot reach fails `fit` with `InvalidParameter { name: "tau", .. }` and stores nothing: `Clayton` on negatively dependent data is an error, never a clamped parameter. Where θ has no finite end the inversion stops at a numerical cap (θ = 10⁴ for `Frank` and `Joe`), a hair short of the table's τ = 1.

### Tail shape

Zero tails in both directions do not imply radial symmetry: `Amh` (below $\theta = 1$) is not radially symmetric, while `Fgm`, `Frank`, `Gaussian`, `Plackett`, `TCopula` and `Independence` are. `Gumbel` and `Joe` share $\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\theta}$, both with $\theta \ge 1$, so tail dependence alone does not choose between them; they differ in the generator, $(-\ln t)^\theta$ against $-\ln(1-(1-t)^\theta)$, and so in the dependence away from the tails.

### Sampling cost

`sample` inverts `partial_derivative` once per pair: in closed form for `Amh`, `Clayton`, `Fgm`, `Frank`, `Gaussian`, `Independence`, `MarshallOlkin`, `Plackett` and `TCopula`, and by Brent's root for `Bb1`, `Bb7`, `Galambos`, `HuslerReiss`, `Gumbel` and `Joe`, the last four taking the identity at their independence value. A fit pays for its τ inversion the same way: `theta_from_tau` is closed-form for `Clayton`, `Fgm`, `Gaussian`, `TCopula`, `MarshallOlkin` and `Gumbel`, and a root-find for `Amh`, `Frank`, `Joe`, `Galambos`, `HuslerReiss` and `Plackett`, the last three integrating their τ at every step.

### What fit expects

Both columns are pseudo-observations strictly inside $(0, 1)$: normalised ranks (`gof::pseudo_observations`) or a fitted marginal cdf (`GaussianUnivariate`). A value outside $[0, 1]$ or a NaN is `MarginalOutOfRange`; a 0 or a 1 is `MarginalOnBoundary`, naming its row, column and value, since ranks $k/(n + 1)$ never reach either end and the densities are NaN there. The uniformity of the columns is not tested: `check_marginal` is a separate diagnostic that no fit runs. `pdf`, `cdf`, `partial_derivative` and `percent_point` share the range rule, and the densities are NaN at exactly 0 or 1.

### The conditioning convention

`partial_derivative` is $\partial_v C(u, v)$, the conditional cdf of `U` given `V = v`, in every family. `percent_point` inverts it in `u` at fixed `v`, and the trait's finite-difference default perturbs the second column, so a family that differentiated the other argument would sample the wrong law without an error.

## Multivariate (`MultivariateExt`)

**6** copulas, one per variant of `multivariate::MultivariateFamily`:

| Copula              | Family              | Notes                                                  |
|----------------------|---------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| GaussianMultivariate | Elliptical          | `try_new(corr)` validates the correlation matrix        |
| TMultivariate        | Elliptical          | Multivariate Student-t                                  |
| NestedArchimedean    | Archimedean, nested | Outer + per-cluster inner generators; `fit` learns the binary tree from Kendall's τ (Górecki–Hofert–Holeňa) |
| CVine                | Vine, PCC           | Canonical vine — genuine pair-copula construction        |
| DVine                | Vine, PCC           | D-vine — genuine pair-copula construction                |
| RVine                | Vine, PCC           | Regular vine — graph-theoretic generalisation of C-/D-vine |

Every multivariate `cdf` is deterministic: Genz's separation of variables over a Sobol set for the Gaussian, t and vine copulas (exact closed forms for the bivariate Gaussian and t), and a closed form for the nested Archimedean copula.

The multivariate copulas need no feature flag; the linear algebra is the pure-Rust `faer`.

## Examples

### Clayton — lower-tail dependence

$\theta > 0$ produces lower-tail dependence, useful for modelling joint crashes in equity returns.

import stochastic_rs as srs

cop = srs.Clayton(theta=2.0)
uv = cop.sample(10_000, seed=42)              # shape (10_000, 2)
tau = srs.kendall_tau_matrix(uv)[0, 1]        # the off-diagonal of the 2×2 tau matrix
print("Kendall's tau ≈", tau)                 # ≈ 0.5

### BB1 / BB7 — two-parameter tail asymmetry

The one-parameter Archimedean families tie the two tails together: Clayton has only lower-tail, Gumbel only upper-tail dependence. BB1 (Clayton–Gumbel) and BB7 (Joe–Clayton) carry two parameters $(\theta, \delta)$ and set the two tails separately — BB1 has $\lambda_L = 2^{-1/(\theta\delta)}$, $\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\delta}$; BB7 has $\lambda_U = 2 - 2^{1/\theta}$, $\lambda_L = 2^{-1/\delta}$. `fit` is a two-parameter maximum-likelihood estimate (Nelder–Mead on the log-density); `theta_from_tau` inverts Kendall's τ at the current δ (closed form for BB1, $\tau = 1 - 2/(\delta(\theta+2))$; numerical for BB7).

`gof::gof_cramer_von_mises` is the Rosenblatt-transform Cramér–von Mises test of Genest, Rémillard & Beaudoin (2009) with a parametric bootstrap p-value and a rejection at `alpha` (`GofConfig`) — the valid procedure when the copula parameters were estimated on the same sample. `multivariate::fit::fit_vine` selects a D-vine or C-vine structure on Kendall's τ (Dißmann et al. 2013) and the family of every edge by AIC or BIC among Independence, Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, Frank, BB1 and BB7, each fitted by maximum likelihood, with the h-functions of the fitted pairs producing the pseudo-observations of the next tree — `DVine::fit`, `CVine::fit` and `RVine::fit` run the same selection with every candidate family and AIC, and the fitted vine evaluates and samples in the data's column order (`order()` says which column each vine variable reads). Feed every fit and test normalised ranks (`gof::pseudo_observations`, `srs.pseudo_observations` in Python): the statistics are rank statistics, and every parametric fit and `gof_cramer_von_mises` refuse a 0 or a 1 with `MarginalOnBoundary`.

<Tabs items={['Rust', 'Python']}>
<Tab value="Rust">
<RustExample path="tests/doctest_copulas_bb1_vine.rs" />
</Tab>
<Tab value="Python">
```python
import numpy as np
import stochastic_rs as srs

truth = srs.Bb1(theta=0.8, delta=1.6)
uv = srs.pseudo_observations(truth.sample(2_000, seed=42))
lower, upper = truth.tail_dependence()      # asymmetric: ≈ 0.58 vs ≈ 0.46

fitted = srs.Bb1()
fitted.fit(uv)                              # MLE of (theta, delta)
stat, p = srs.copula_gof("bb1", uv, replications=50, seed=7)
print(fitted.theta(), fitted.delta(), p)    # p well above 0.05

# 3-column data: structure + family selection with BIC
u3 = srs.pseudo_observations(np.column_stack([uv, np.random.default_rng(1).uniform(size=2_000)]))
fit = srs.fit_vine(u3, structure="dvine", criterion="bic")
print(fit["order"], fit["families"], fit["aic"])
```
